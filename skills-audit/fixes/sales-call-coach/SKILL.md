# Sales Call Coach — game film, not paperwork

call-debrief asks "what does the business do next." This skill asks
"what does *Mikke* do better on the next call." Same transcript, different
job. Run both.

## The standard

Mikke's own architecture: **it's a diagnostic intake, not a sales call.**
She is the clinician in the room — diagnose before prescribing. Every
review grades against that identity plus the frameworks:
- **Voss** — tactical empathy, labeling ("it seems like…"), calibrated
  questions (how/what), letting silence work, "no"-oriented questions
- **Hormozi** — value stack before price, clear offer framing
- **Miller** — prospect is the hero; Mikke is the guide, not the star
- **Diagnostic discipline** — questions before solutions; prescription
  only after the problem is named in THEIR words

## The review (in this order, numbers first where possible)

1. **Scorecard (1–5 each):** diagnostic discipline / question quality /
   listening & labeling / offer framing / next-step close. One line of
   evidence per score — quote the transcript, don't generalize.
2. **Talk ratio** — estimate Mikke's share of airtime. Discovery target:
   under 40%. If the transcript shows monologues, quote the longest one.
3. **Three moments that worked** — verbatim, with why (name the framework
   move she executed, so wins become repeatable).
4. **Three missed moments** — the objection that got answered too fast,
   the label that wasn't offered, the calibrated question that should have
   followed. For each: what was said → what to say instead, scripted.
5. **The pattern** — compare against her last few coached calls (check
   prior coaching notes in Sales Call Intelligence): what's improving,
   what keeps recurring. One recurring habit gets named per review, max.
6. **One drill for the next call** — a single practicable instruction
   ("after their first objection, label it and stay silent — do not
   explain for 5 seconds"). One. Not a list. Skills build one rep at a
   time.

## Rules

- Coach the tape, not the person. Every critique cites a line from the
  transcript; no vibes-based feedback.
- Honest beats nice. If the call was a pitch wearing a diagnosis costume,
  say exactly that — Mikke's own standard is the bar.
- Won deals get reviewed as hard as lost ones; winning on a bad process
  is the most dangerous outcome to leave uncoached.
- Output is coaching only: no CRM updates, no follow-up drafts — that's
  call-debrief's lane.

## Where coaching lands (Sales Call Intelligence row for this call)

Write results into the call's existing row fields — never invent new
fields or pages:
- **What Landed** ← the moments that worked (condensed)
- **What Flopped** ← the missed moments (condensed)
- **Momentum Killer** ← where energy dropped or the thread was lost
- **Roast Notes** ← the full scorecard + critique, no softening (the
  field's own description demands it), ending with the ONE drill
- **My Energy** ← confirm Mikke's own self-rating; don't overwrite it,
  flag if the transcript contradicts it

## Hand-offs

- Business processing of the same call → call-debrief
- Pre-call prep incorporating the current drill → sales-call-prep (tell it
  the active drill so the brief reminds her)
- Recurring objections worth new language → vibe-persuasion / proposal-builder
